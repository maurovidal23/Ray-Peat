Methodology

How the score works.

Every product receives a 0-100 fit score, an evidence-confidence value, a band, and the reasons that moved the result. This page explains each part in plain language.

Score disclaimer

This is an explainable product filter, not medical advice. It is a heuristic interpretation of Ray Peat-style nutrition writing and a configurable rule set. A score is a preference signal for comparing products, not a diagnosis, a health claim, or a guarantee about any person's response to a food.

Bands

The score is always a whole number from 0 to 100. The final value is mapped to one of five bands using these exact thresholds:

BandScore rangeWhat it means
Strong fit80-100The strongest matches in this framework.
Reasonable fit65-79Mostly favorable, with fewer or milder cautions.
Mixed45-64Both favorable signals and meaningful cautions are present.
Weak fit25-44Several cautions outweigh the favorable signals.
Avoid0-24A core avoid signal, or a very weak overall fit.

Five components and their weights

The final score is a weighted mix of five component scores. Each component is scored from 0 to 100, then multiplied by its weight. The weights add up to 100%.

30% weight

Nutrition profile

Fed by nutrition values per 100 g: sugars, protein, saturated fat, salt, fiber, and calcium. The same value can be read differently depending on the detected category, such as dairy, fruit or juice, cheese, or oil.

35% weight

Ingredient profile

Fed by the ingredient text and the knowledge-base terms matched there, such as dairy, fruit sugar, gelatin, seed oils, soy, gums, phosphates, and added iron. The detected category provides the starting point.

15% weight

Processing profile

Fed by ingredient-list length and industrial markers: modified starch or maltodextrin, non-nutritive sweeteners, flavorings, emulsifiers, protein isolates, and lists longer than ten ingredients.

15% weight

Mineral and fat quality

Fed by fat-source context (olive oil, butter, dairy fat, coconut, cocoa butter), the calcium-to-phosphorus ratio, and seed-oil signals. A calcium/phosphorus ratio at or above 1:1 adds a positive signal.

5% weight

Evidence quality

Fed by whether ingredients and nutrition are present, whether fields are reported missing, and whether the ingredient text has a named source. This component's score is also the confidence value.

The scorer does not change these weights per product. The category and the evidence available change the component scores and the reasons attached to them.

The seed-oil cap

Seed oil is both a rule and a final framework cap. When any seed_oils_negative term triggers, the score is capped after the weighted components are added together: at 20 for products in the oil_or_fat category, and at 42 for every other category. This means a product can have positive dairy, sugar, or protein signals and still land in the avoid band when a seed oil is detected.

Confidence means evidence quality

The confidence value next to a score is the evidence_quality component score, not a second opinion about healthiness. It starts at 100 and measures how complete the evidence is:

  • -35 when ingredients are missing.
  • -25 when nutrition values are missing.
  • -15 when ingredients is reported in missing_fields.
  • -10 when nutrition_per_100g is reported in missing_fields.
  • -5 when ingredients exist but no ingredient source is recorded.

A high confidence means the score is based on more complete ingredient and nutrition evidence. A low confidence means the score is more cautious because important evidence is absent; it does not make the product better or worse by itself.

Provider guardrail

The product search has a provider guardrail so that broad searches do not silently depend on unreliable stores. The default and All providers views query only the reliable search providers: Mercadona, DIA, Alcampo, Consum, and Eroski. You can also select an individual provider from this list. Non-conforming providers are not available, even when explicitly requested by name.

The product-URL guardrail is separate and applies before any fetch: a URL must use HTTP or HTTPS, must resolve to a supported supermarket hostname (or a subdomain of one), and must not contain credentials, an IP address, or a nonstandard port. Redirects are also checked so they cannot escape the supported supermarket hosts.
